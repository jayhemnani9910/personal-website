  architecture: "Three encoder branches feed one fusion layer. ProtT5 encodes the amino-acid sequence, a 1D SpectralCNN encodes the VDOS spectrum, and a ChemBERTa-plus-fingerprint encoder handles substrate SMILES. The VDOS spectrum is produced upstream: a structure goes through ANM or GNM normal mode analysis with ProDy, the eigenvalues are converted to vibrational frequencies, and Lorentzian broadening turns the discrete modes into a continuous 1000-point spectrum. TriModalFusion projects all three branches to a shared dimension and combines them with softmax gate weights from a small gating network, so each modality's contribution is learned per sample. A regression head maps the fused vector to log(k_cat), and the gate weights are returned alongside for inspection. The Quantum Data Decoder core also carries graph (GATv2) and CNN encoders and Novozymes/CAFA5 dataset loaders for the stability and function tasks."

  keyDecisions:
    - decision: "Add a vibrational (VDOS) feature as a third modality"
      reasoning: "Sequence and chemistry are standard inputs; conformational dynamics are not. Normal mode analysis gives a cheap, structure-derived spectrum that captures flexibility and hinge motion, a signal that static representations cannot express."
      alternatives: "Sequence and chemistry only (simpler, but blind to dynamics); full molecular dynamics (far too expensive to run per protein at dataset scale)."
    - decision: "Gated fusion instead of plain concatenation"
      reasoning: "The three modalities are not equally informative for every protein, and one may be missing entirely. A softmax gating network lets the model weight branches per sample and exposes those weights for interpretation."
      alternatives: "Concatenate-and-MLP (no per-sample weighting), bilinear fusion (more parameters, harder to read)."
    - decision: "MM-Drop: randomly drop the spectral branch during training"
      reasoning: "VDOS needs a structure, which is not always available at inference time. Masking the spectral modality during training forces the sequence and chemistry branches to stay predictive on their own, so a structureless query still returns a prediction."
      alternatives: "Require all three modalities (brittle in deployment), or train separate single-modality models (loses the benefit of fusion)."

        concat = torch.cat([h_seq, h_spec, h_chem], dim=-1)
        gates = torch.softmax(self.gate_net(concat), dim=-1)  # (batch, 3)

        proj_seq = self.proj_seq(h_seq)
        proj_spec = self.proj_spec(h_spec)
        proj_chem = self.proj_chem(h_chem)

        fused = (
            gates[:, 0:1] * proj_seq
            + gates[:, 1:2] * proj_spec
            + gates[:, 2:3] * proj_chem
        )
        return self.dropout(fused), gates
      explanation: "A small gating network reads all three embeddings and emits softmax weights over the sequence, spectral, and chemical branches. Each branch is projected to a shared dimension and summed by its gate, so the fusion is a learned per-sample weighting rather than a fixed concatenation. Nobel Data Intelligence is a physics-informed deep learning framework for protein stability and enzyme kinetics. It encodes a protein through three branches, ProtT5 over the sequence, a SpectralCNN over a VDOS vibrational spectrum derived from normal mode analysis, and ChemBERTa plus reaction fingerprints over substrate chemistry, then combines them with a learned gating layer to predict properties such as catalytic turnover (k_cat). It is built in two phases: a Quantum Data Decoder core for general molecular property prediction and VibroPredict on top for enzyme kinetics, with unit-tested subsystems, eight notebooks, and an interactive demo that renders the VDOS spectrum live.
